Journey · Step 2

Build your voice

Confidence comes from regular, low-pressure practice. Start with people who are happy to listen.

You don't need perfect English to start. You need a safe place to practice.

The activities below are free or very low cost, welcoming, and made for people exactly where you are now. Pick one that fits your week — showing up regularly matters more than speaking perfectly.

Tips from someone who has been there

Habits that helped a Chinese international student — who started at the UTAS Language Centre — make English feel normal.

Your English is better than you think

Exams train reading and listening, so speaking lags behind — that is normal for almost everyone. Speaking improves through repetition, not more grammar study.

Practise in tiny moments

Say one extra sentence to the barista, the bus driver or the cashier each day. After a month, small talk stops being scary.

One regular group beats random chats

Pick a weekly group and keep going back. Familiar faces and repeated topics make talking much easier than meeting strangers every time.

Keep your circle, expand your voice

It's fine to find activities through Chinese-speaking friends — just agree to speak English once you're there. And don't worry about your accent: Hobart locals are patient and used to many accents.
